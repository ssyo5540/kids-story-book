---
title: Ganesha and the Race Around the World
summary: Two brothers race around the whole world for a single golden mango, and the slower one finds a much shorter way.
moral: The people you love are your whole world.

---

High on a snowy mountain, in a home surrounded by clouds, lived a family of gods. There was Shiva, the father, who was quiet and strong. There was Parvati, the mother, who was warm and wise. And there were two brothers who could not have been more different.

The elder was Kartikeya. He was quick and bright and brave, and he rode a peacock with feathers like blue fire. The younger was Ganesha. He was round and gentle and thoughtful, with the head of an elephant and a little mouse for a friend, and he liked to do things slowly, thinking all the way.

One afternoon a traveller came up the mountain and gave the family a gift. It was a mango. But it was no ordinary mango. It was golden, and it glowed softly, and it smelled like every sweet thing in the world at once.

"This is the fruit of wisdom," said the traveller. "Whoever eats it will understand the whole world." And then he went on his way.

Both brothers looked at the mango. Both brothers wanted it very badly.

"There is only one," said Parvati. "We cannot cut it, or the magic will be spoiled."

Shiva thought for a moment. Then he smiled. "Let us have a race," he said. "Whichever of you travels all the way around the world and comes back first shall have the mango."

Kartikeya did not wait to hear another word. He leaped onto his peacock, and the peacock spread its shining wings, and off they flew, faster than the wind, over the edge of the mountain and away.

Ganesha did not move.

He looked at his round tummy. He looked at his short legs. He looked at his little mouse, who was very brave but also very small. Then he looked at his mother and father, sitting side by side on the mountain, and he began to think.

Far away, Kartikeya flew over deserts. He flew over jungles. He flew over the great blue sea, where whales looked up at him. He flew over cities and villages and rivers, and everywhere he went, people pointed and said, "Look, how fast!"

Back on the mountain, Ganesha finished thinking. He stood up. He folded his hands. And he began, very slowly, to walk around his parents.

Once around. He bowed to his mother.

Twice around. He bowed to his father.

Three times around. He bowed to them both. Then he sat down again exactly where he had started, and patted his mouse on the head.

"There," he said. "I have finished."

Shiva raised his eyebrows. Parvati tried not to smile.

"Finished what?" said Shiva. "You have not gone anywhere at all."

"I have gone around the world," said Ganesha. "You are my mother and my father. You made me. You feed me and teach me and love me. Whatever is in the world, it is in you first. So when I walk around you, I walk around everything."

There was a long, quiet moment on the mountain. The clouds drifted. Ganesha's mouse twitched his whiskers.

Then Parvati laughed out loud, the warmest laugh in all the heavens, and Shiva laughed too, deep and slow like faraway thunder.

"That," said Shiva, "is wisdom." And he placed the golden mango in Ganesha's hands.

Just then there was a rush of wind and a flash of blue, and Kartikeya landed on the mountain, breathless and dusty, with his peacock panting behind him.

"I have done it!" he cried. "I have been all the way around!" Then he saw the mango in his brother's lap, and he stopped.

For a moment his face fell. But Kartikeya, though he was quick, was not unkind. He listened while his parents explained. He looked at his round little brother, who had not moved from the mountain. And slowly he began to grin.

"You clever old elephant," he said.

Ganesha broke the mango carefully in two, and the magic did not spoil at all, because a gift that is shared is never spoiled. He gave the larger half to his brother.

"You did see the world," he said. "You can tell me about it."

So Kartikeya sat down, and he told his brother about the deserts and the whales and the cities, until the sun went down and the stars came out over the snow.

And Ganesha listened, with sticky golden fingers, leaning against his mother, until his eyes closed, and the whole wide world was safe inside his dreams.
