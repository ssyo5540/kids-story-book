title: The Little Squirrel Who Helped Build the Bridge
summary: When the monkeys build a bridge across the sea, one tiny squirrel decides that she will help too, one pebble at a time.
moral: No helper is too small to matter.

On a wide sandy shore, where the land ended and the blue sea began, there was a great deal of noise.

Prince Rama and his friend Hanuman had come to the sea with a whole army of monkeys and bears. They needed to reach an island far away across the water, and there was no boat big enough. So they had decided to build a bridge.

The monkeys were very strong. They lifted huge rocks and tossed them into the waves. The bears rolled great boulders down from the hills. Splash! Splash! Splash! Little by little, a road of stone began to stretch out across the sea.

Now, in a tamarind tree near the shore, there lived a small brown squirrel. She had a fluffy tail and a twitchy nose, and she watched everything.

She watched the monkeys carry rocks. She watched Hanuman lift a stone as big as a house. She watched Rama walking up and down the shore, thanking every single worker by name.

"I want to help too," said the squirrel.

She scampered down the tree. She looked at the rocks. They were far too big for her. Even the smallest one was bigger than her whole body.

So she looked at the ground instead. There, among the shells, were pebbles. Small, round, grey pebbles. And there was sand, fine and golden.

The squirrel picked up one pebble in her mouth. She ran to the water's edge, dropped it onto the stone road, and ran back for another. Then she rolled in the sand until her fur was full of it, ran to the bridge, and shook herself so that the sand fell into the cracks between the rocks.

Back and forth she went. Pebble, sand, pebble, sand. Her legs grew tired. Her tail drooped. But she did not stop.

Some of the monkeys noticed her. "Look at that," one of them laughed. "A squirrel building a bridge! Move aside, tiny one, before you get squashed."

The squirrel moved aside. But then she went right back to work.

Rama had been watching. He walked over quietly, and he knelt down on the sand so that his face was close to hers.

"What are you doing, little one?" he asked.

"Helping," said the squirrel, a bit out of breath. "The pebbles fill the gaps between the big rocks. The sand fills the gaps between the pebbles. It makes the bridge strong."

Rama looked at the bridge. The squirrel was right. Where her pebbles and sand had gone, the great stones fitted snugly together, and the waves could not push them apart.

He turned to the monkeys. "Do you see?" he said. "The big rocks are the bridge. But the small pebbles are what hold it together. We could not do this without her."

The monkeys stopped laughing. One of them bowed to the squirrel. Then another. Then all of them.

Rama lifted the squirrel gently and stroked her back with three fingers, from her head to her tail, the way you would stroke a sleepy kitten. Where his fingers passed, three soft pale stripes appeared in her brown fur.

"So that everyone will always remember," he said, "that you helped."

That is why, to this very day, squirrels in that land have three stripes down their backs.

By evening the bridge reached all the way to the island. The monkeys and bears sat down on the shore, tired and happy. Hanuman lay on his back and counted stars.

And in the tamarind tree, the little squirrel curled up in her nest with sand still between her toes. She tucked her striped back against the warm bark, closed her eyes, and dreamed of pebbles, one after another, rolling gently into place.
